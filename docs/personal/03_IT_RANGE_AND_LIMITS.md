## My short answer

**Put me in almost any software-facing IT environment, give me a real problem, access to the relevant system and time to understand the domain, and I can usually find a useful contribution.**

**Hardware engineering, physical electronics and device repair are not my specialisation.** I'm not saying I could never learn them, just that my demonstrated work is overwhelmingly in data and software systems.

The distinction matters: **broad problem-solving ability is not the same as already being fully qualified for every specialist role**. I wouldn't claim that because I can build APIs I am automatically a specialist kernel engineer, a certified penetration tester or a senior network architect.

## Why breadth has practical value

When a reporting issue appears, it might live in SQL, a dataflow, an API, access controls, a DAX calculation or a browser interaction. Somebody who can investigate more than one layer can narrow the fault before escalating.

When a new product is designed, the same cross-stack view helps identify ownership and integration risks early: where an event originates, who can mutate it, how a user sees it, and what happens when a dependency fails.

I have been the person working across those boundaries in professional and independent projects.

## Where breadth can become a weakness

I do not want to use versatility as an excuse to be vague or skip depth. Specialist systems deserve specialist review. My contribution can be to understand the interface, diagnose the problem, design the integration or help coordinate a safe solution, without pretending I have personally run every class of infrastructure.

**The strongest version of my career story is not “I know everything”. It is “I know how to learn, investigate, connect systems and deliver across several important engineering domains.”**

## A practical interview answer

> I am strongest in data engineering, analytics engineering and full-stack software delivery, with hands-on experience stretching into applied AI, security-minded development, cloud and DevOps. I've worked through source data, databases, APIs, interfaces, testing and deployments rather than only one layer. That makes me adaptable across most software-oriented IT teams. I'm not positioning myself as a hardware engineer or an instant expert in every specialist niche. I bring a strong foundation, practical problem-solving and the discipline to close the skill gaps a role requires.

## Where I'm going

I want to grow toward solutions architecture and technology leadership because I enjoy the responsibilities between disciplines: business value, system design, risk, cost, team coordination and long-term reliability.

**Breadth gets me into the conversation. Evidence and sound judgment keep me there.**
