# Chapter 09 | How I work and where I am going

[← Previous](08_REPRODUCE_THE_WORK.md) · [Contents](../OMNIMASTER_HANDBOOK.md) · [Next →](10_GLOSSARY_AND_EVIDENCE.md)

My ambition is to grow toward **solution architecture, principal engineering and technology leadership**, ultimately earning the scope and judgment required of a CTO. I am not presenting a future title as a current one. I am interested in the responsibility: making better technical decisions, developing reliable teams and owning the consequences.

## The kind of engineer I am

I have often worked in places where the reporting problem is a database problem, the database problem is a business-definition problem, and the business-definition problem cannot be solved without a conversation with the people who operate the process.

That shapes my delivery style:

**I investigate.** Before replacing technology, I inspect source contracts, actual errors, query plans, runtime limits and constraints.

**I implement.** SQL, APIs, UI, background jobs, tests and deployment are connected work. I aim to understand where the change will have an effect.

**I communicate.** Non-technical users need to know whether the numbers are correct and when they will change, not the complete stack trace.

**I document.** If a feature is a draft, an infrastructure cutover is staged or an AI provider is unavailable, the status should be visible.

**I protect trust.** I prefer clear refusals, tenant-aware permissions, reversible migrations and honest release status over a convenient but unsafe shortcut.

## Working with business, product and engineering

| Situation | How I approach it |
|---|---|
| Conflicting KPI definitions | Ask for one agreed business event, population, time window and owner; write tests and examples |
| Stakeholder wants a faster report | Reproduce and measure the bottleneck, preserve correctness, communicate the change |
| Product request spans multiple layers | Draw the flow, define contracts, identify owner of state transitions, split verifiable work |
| Security finding appears | Validate its mechanism with source and a test; distinguish a scanner observation from a vulnerability |
| Deadline threatens release quality | State what is verified, what is blocked, and what reversible scope can safely ship |
| AI-generated output influences people | Use bounded assistance, provenance, human review and a correction path |

The skill I want to sharpen most is **technical judgment under real constraints**: balancing reliability, cost, time, team capability, law, security and customer value.

| Role family | Why it fits | The growth edge I am developing |

| **Data Engineer / Data Platform Engineer** | SQL, ETL/ELT, source contracts, modelling, performance and pipeline ownership | Broader streaming, distributed orchestration and large-scale operations |

| **Solution Architect pathway** | Can connect business goals to data, applications and security requirements | Documented architecture leadership, cross-team decisions and mentoring |

This is a map of relevant work and developing strengths, not a claim to have served in all of these titles.

## What I would like a technical interview to explore

I would rather discuss one system deeply than recite definitions.

A meaningful conversation could examine a real-world source-change problem, why a dashboard went stale, how to avoid duplicate financial transactions, how to protect learners across institutions, or how a model-assisted grading workflow could fail safely.

A strong discussion might start with:

> How would you trace a discrepancy from a visual all the way back to the source event, without hiding it behind another validation layer?

That question captures much of my experience.

## The direction of the next chapter of my career

I want to lead toward systems that are **useful, scalable where necessary, cost-aware, secure and explainable to their users**. I'm developing independent technology products partly because shipping complete experiences exposes gaps that a narrowly scoped reporting task may never reveal.

I am also learning to distinguish between *building the feature* and *building the organisation that can safely maintain it*. Effective technical leadership needs architecture, documentation, hiring and mentorship, reliable delivery processes and the confidence to say no to unsafe shortcuts.

I do not want to become detached from the code. I want to keep enough depth to understand the trade-off behind the decision.
